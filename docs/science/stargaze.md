# Stargaze: what the channel shows, and what the game should take from it

The user asked (1 Nov 2026) for the YouTube channel **Stargaze** (@Stargaze_youtube, 148 videos,
SpaceEngine simulations) to be used as reference for the gas giants, moons, atmospheres,
weather and temperatures, singling out the *Falling Into* series.

**How it was studied.** YouTube asks this cloud machine to sign in before it serves video, and
the user's rule is never to ask them to sign in, so the videos were not downloaded. What YouTube
serves publicly was used instead: every video's title and length, its thumbnail, and its three
automatic preview frames (about a quarter, half and three quarters through). The 54 videos below
were looked at frame by frame; the other 94 (exoplanets, galaxies, size comparisons, the end of
the universe) only by title, since none of them is a place in the game. The frames are kept in
the session scratchpad, not in this repository (third-party images).

To look at any of them properly, open `https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=<id>`.

## What the videos do that the game should copy

1. **A number on screen at each stage.** "Altitude 24 km", "Wind speed 2,000 km/h",
   "Temperature: 15,000,000 °C", "The temperature rapidly climbs to over 300 °C". ARGO already
   reads numbers out; the HUD can carry an altitude chip and the one figure that matters there.
2. **Weather you can see on the glass.** Rain on the visor on Titan, ice grains streaking past
   inside Saturn and Uranus, particles hitting the canopy. It sells being inside the air.
3. **Things in front of other things.** Phobos crossing Mars, ring boulders black against
   Saturn, Pluto ringed in blue against the Sun. Silhouettes against a bright world.
4. **One fact per scene.** Short captions, plain words, one number each.

## Order of work (each measured for frame time before it ships)

1. **Done (1 Nov 2026):** eclipses. eclipse.ts works out how much of the Sun any world covers from the camera; the world's shared sunlight (sky, ground, grass, trees, clouds) dims by that share, the eyes adapt, the lens ghosts go, and in totality the Sun's glare becomes the corona: pearly streamers to three radii, red prominences at the limb. The Totality expedition sends the player into the Moon's shadow in space (from the ground at the Moon's mean distance it is a ring of fire, as it really is).
2. **Done (1 Nov 2026):** backlit haze rings. Seen from space, each world's air scatters sunlight from behind it forwards (a Henyey-Greenstein phase) along a Chapman grazing path, per colour: blue is scattered most, so it rings the top of the air and is lost lower down, where what gets through reddens. Earth from the Moon's side shows a thin red-orange ring edged blue, Pluto a thin blue line, Titan an orange halo, Mars a pale rim. The sky's own glow round the sun is now drawn only from inside the air, and air beyond the terminator seen from space is lit only by what crosses the limb. Same frame time at identical views.
3. Titan: the blue detached haze; rain on the canopy.
4. Ring-plane boulder density at Saturn; ice grains streaking inside Saturn and Uranus.
5. Triton's nitrogen geysers (reuse EnceladusGeysers); Pluto's methane snow.
6. Europa under the ice: the hydrothermal vent with life (already on the Next list).
7. Neptune's wind streaks with the 2,000 km/h figure.
