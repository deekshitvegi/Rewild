# Rewild: game design

Version 1, 8 Nov 2026. Written after the owner's verdict on the web version ("there's nothing interesting going on
in the game other than the exploring the space part... I wanted space wars... The story you mentioned is really
short though I want building type of game and also battle kind of game too!!"). Engine: Unreal Engine 5.

## In one paragraph

Earth has been empty for 300 years, but not abandoned: the Harrow, a species that strips dead worlds, found it and
are grinding its cities into ore. Four crew come home from Haven, a colony out by the black hole TON 618, to bring
Earth back to life. They find it occupied, a few thousand people still holding out underground, and a Harrow armada
on its way. Rewild is a war you fight in the cockpit, aboard enemy ships and on the ground, and a civilisation you
build out of what you win: bases on every world, factories and shipyards, a fleet, and in the end living cities on
a living Earth.

## The four pillars

1. **Fight for every world.** Dogfights, capital ship battles, boarding, ground battles. Nothing is handed over.
2. **Build what you win.** Every world taken from the Harrow becomes a base you design and run. What you build is
   what defends it, and what it makes arms the next battle.
3. **People who matter.** The crew, the Holdouts on Earth, the colonists on Haven, the alien allies: each has
   needs, a story and a say. Your choices cost lives or save them.
4. **Real places, real science, top visuals.** The real Solar System and its real figures, the Seed Vault at 78° N,
   Shackleton's rim, Jezero. Every place built to be walked into.

## The loop

```
   scout a world ──► battle for it ──► claim it ──► build a base ──► it produces
        ▲                                                             │
        │      the Harrow strike back at what you built ◄─────────────┤
        └──────────── supplies, ships and allies for the next world ◄─┘
```

The war map shows every world: Harrow-held, contested or yours. Bases make resources, ships and people; convoys
carry them between worlds and can be raided. The Harrow answer what you take: they hit your weakest base, your
convoys, your allies. Two attacks at once means choosing which to defend.

## Battle

| Kind | You | What it plays like |
|---|---|---|
| **Dogfights** | Your fighter; the other crew as wingmen you give orders to | Guns that need leading, lock-on missiles, flares, shields that recharge, a boost with heat. Harrow fighters fly in packs and flank. |
| **Capital ships** | The Argo, then your fleet | Man a turret or take the bridge and give orders (focus fire, screen the carrier, launch bombers). Weak points: drives, hangars, shield emitters. Ships break apart where they are hit. |
| **Boarding** | On foot with a squad | Dock or breach, fight corridor by corridor, cut power, open doors, take the bridge or the core. Every ship you board you could keep. |
| **Ground battles** | On foot, with squads, rovers and walkers | Take or hold a place. Cover, flanking, height. Call fighters down from orbit for a strike run. |
| **Base defence** | Everything you built | Turrets, walls, shield domes, missile batteries and your garrison against waves. Built badly, it falls. |

Weapons and gadgets are made, not found: rifles, launchers, grenades, a grappling line, deployable cover and the
**stasis lance** (Amara builds it from Maker technology: it wraps a target in slowed time for a few seconds; it works
on Harrow soldiers, on a falling ship and on a geyser about to blow). Allies add their own: Europan shields of
living light, aeronaut lightning, Holdout snipers.

Losing costs something real: a destroyed base is rebuilt from what is left, a lost ship is gone, a crew member can
be badly wounded and out for a chapter. Nobody you care about dies by accident; deaths in the story are choices.

## Building

Five layers, unlocked in this order:

1. **Kit.** Suit, fighter and weapons upgraded at a fabricator from salvage and crystals.
2. **Bases.** Placed module by module on any ground (or hung from the clouds on Venus): extractors, refineries,
   power (solar, fission, then fusion on helium-3 from Jupiter), habitats, greenhouses, hangars, shipyards, comms,
   and defences (turrets, walls, shield domes, missile batteries). Modules snap together and are built by drones
   you watch working. People staff them and need air, water, food, warmth, light and something to live for.
3. **Fleet.** Shipyards build fighters, bombers, corvettes, frigates and finally carriers. The Argo is refitted
   module by module (hangar bays, turrets, armour, drives). Captured Harrow ships can be rebuilt as yours.
4. **Supply.** Freighter routes between worlds, escorted or raided. A factory with nothing coming in stops.
5. **Earth.** After the liberation: towns, then cities, built district by district (homes, farms, power,
   workshops, schools, a spaceport), and the land around them rewilded with the Seven Seeds: forests planted and
   growing over years, rivers cleaned, animals brought back from the Seed Vault's frozen embryos. The planet you
   see from orbit changes as you work.

Each world gives its own building material and look: Moon regolith blocks, Martian iron, Europan ice and living
coral, aeronaut silk balloons, Titan's hydrocarbons, Harrow alloy torn from their wrecks.

## The people

**The crew**, each with a battle role, a building role and a story of their own (side missions, a choice at the
end of each act):

- **Dev Arora**, pilot. Leads the wing. Was born on Haven and has never seen Earth.
- **Noor Haddad**, botanist and medic. Runs the greenhouses; the Seven Seeds are her life's work.
- **Kai Mendes**, engineer. Builds everything; unlocks base modules and refits the Argo.
- **Amara Okafor**, physicist. Reads the Makers' science; builds the stasis lance and the late-game weapons.
- **ARGO**, the ship's mind: plans with you, warns you, talks to the crew; never a narrator.

**The Holdouts.** About 3,000 people who never left Earth, living in deep shelters for 300 years (the Seed Vault's
tunnels, a mountain bunker, a flooded metro). Proud, wary, divided: some want to fight, some want to stay hidden,
one faction trades with the Harrow. They become your soldiers, pilots, builders and, in the end, the first
citizens of the new Earth.

**Haven.** 400 colonists by a black hole, where one day is ten years on Earth. Every month you spend on Earth is
seconds there; every hour you spend on Haven costs Earth years. Bringing them home is the last act.

**Allies**, each won by a battle and a choice, each adding a branch to what you can build:

- **The Europans** (under Europa's ice; speak in light). Held as labour in a Harrow mining station. Freed, they
  give living-coral habitats, healing and shields of light.
- **The Aeronauts** (Venus's clouds; talk in radio pulses and prime numbers). Their floating city is under attack.
  Saved, they give airship hulls for your capital ships and lightning weapons.
- **The Titan hunters** (methane seas). They hunt the Harrow too. Trusted, they give stealth and Titan fuel.
- **Vesk**, a Harrow deserter, a worker caste that refused to grind one more world. Your guide inside their ships.
  Whether to trust it is the player's call, more than once.
- **The Makers**, long gone, who built the wormhole and rewilded worlds before. Their archive on Triton explains the
  Harrow and holds the last weapon.

## The Harrow

A hive that eats dead worlds. Castes: **drones** (workers, crawlers that grind cities), **reapers** (fighters),
**wardens** (soldiers), **barges** (ore haulers), **carriers** (motherships, each holding orbit over a world) and
the **Overseer** that speaks for them. Their ships and buildings are dark alloy with hot seams where the ore runs.
They do not hate people; to them Earth was empty and therefore theirs. They learn: they copy your tactics, raid
what you leave weak, and from Act II they send for the armada.

## The campaign

About 40-60 hours for the story, then open play. Five acts; each act is a set of worlds on the war map, several
missions each, in an order the player partly chooses.

### Prologue: *Ten Years a Day* (Haven)
- The colony by TON 618; Haven's orbit is failing. Walk the shrine garden, the domes, meet the council.
- First building: a landing pad and a hangar (the building tutorial). First flight: drone training over the sea.
- A Harrow probe comes out of the wormhole by Haven and is shot down: they know where people are. The crew takes
  the Argo through. If the Harrow come through in force, Haven dies.

### Act I: *Blockade* (Earth and the Moon)
1. **Breakout.** Out of the wormhole by Saturn, run the picket, dogfight to the Moon. The Argo is hit.
2. **Farside.** Hide on the Moon's far side. Build the first base: regolith extractor, solar array, hangar, repair dock.
3. **First contact.** A Holdout radio call from Svalbard. Raid a Harrow ore barge for parts.
4. **The Vault.** Land under fire at the Seed Vault (78° N), fight into the tunnels: the Harrow are inside, the
   Holdouts are holding the deepest vault. Ground battle and interior.
5. **Svalbard holds.** First base on Earth around the Vault. First base defence: the Harrow strike back.
6. **Eternal Light.** Retake the solar farm on Shackleton's rim at the Moon's south pole; power for everything.
7. **The Overseer speaks.** A warning, and a choice: give up the Vault's seeds and they leave Svalbard alone.

### Act II: *Allies* (the inner and outer worlds; partly in the order the player picks)
- **Mars, *Jezero*:** the abandoned colony and its soil lab, overrun. Ground war across the crater through a dust
  storm. Build: the Mars shipyard (frigates).
- **Europa, *Under the Ice*:** board the Harrow mining station on the ice, go down the crack, free the Europans.
  Build: living-coral habitats under the ice.
- **Venus, *The Aeronauts*:** defend the floating city in a capital ship battle between the cloud decks. Build:
  airship docks, cloud-top refineries.
- **Titan, *Hunters*:** stealth on the methane sea, a hunt for the Harrow scouts with the Titan hunters. Build: fuel
  depot.
- **Jupiter, *Deep*:** dive for helium-3 under the floaters, Harrow gas-mining rigs to take. Build: fusion.
- **Vesk:** the deserter's offer, the first boarding of a carrier's outer decks, and the news: the armada is coming.
- Throughout: convoy escorts, raids on barges, base defences, Holdout politics on Earth.

### Act III: *The Siege of Earth*
1. **Home Fleet.** Everything you have built and every ally against the carrier over Earth.
2. **Mothership.** Board the carrier, fight to its core with the crew and Vesk; bring it down over the Pacific.
3. **The cities.** Ground war on three continents (real ruined cities), Holdouts rising with you, fighters called down
   from orbit. Free each region and found the first towns.
4. **The Makers.** Triton's archive: what the Harrow are, and the Makers' gate weapon. Amara's choice.

### Act IV: *Home*
1. **The Call.** Through the wormhole, land on Haven, convince the council (the ark Halcyon must fly once more).
2. **The ark lands.** 400 people and three centuries of the Holdouts meet. Cities, rewilding and the Seven Seeds.
3. **The Armada.** The Harrow armada arrives. The last battle defends everything you built, in orbit, in the
   cities and in the new forests. How it ends depends on the allies won, the choices made, and what you built.

### After the story
Open play: keep building Earth's cities and wild places, run the Solar System's bases and fleet, and skirmishes
against Harrow remnants that still raid.

## How you play: story, free play, continue (owner, 9 Nov 2026)

"a story mode and a free mode where you can explore without worrying about any missions ... not forcing people to
play story mode only ... and an option to continue the game where you left off". The main menu (Liquid Glass,
sentence case, like the rest of the UI) has: **Continue**, **Story**, **Free play**, Settings, Quit.

- **Story:** the campaign above, mission by mission, choices that cost or save lives.
- **Free play:** the worlds without missions. Fly, explore, build bases, and fight when you choose: Harrow patrols
  still roam and still fight hard (the game is meant to be difficult), but nothing pushes you on. Nobody is made to
  play the story to enjoy the game.
- **Continue:** the game saves by itself at every checkpoint, at each mission's start and end, after building, and
  on quitting; Continue picks up the last game, story or free play, exactly where it was left. Story and free play
  keep separate saves, with a few slots each.
- First forms: the first fight's mission is the first story mission; the endless Reaper packs over Earth (today's
  `-RewildHarrow`) are the first free play; the menu and the saves come with first fight milestone 5.

## What the player sees in the first hour (the bar for the first build)

1. Haven at golden hour, the garden and the colony; build the pad and the hangar.
2. A training dogfight over Haven's sea; the probe comes through; the alarm.
3. The wormhole crossing (made in the engine, from a script).
4. Out at Saturn into the Harrow picket: a real fight, the Argo hit, a run for the Moon with Earth beneath.
5. Farside: the first base, built piece by piece while the crew patches the ship.

If that hour is not exciting and beautiful, nothing after it matters. It is built first.
