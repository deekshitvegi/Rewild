# The story mode: bringing people home

Working title: **Rewild** (the owner picks the real one).

This replaces the old chapter trail (the Obelisk, the portfolio's easter egg). The game is
its own product now: it will ship on Steam, the App Store and Google Play, and it also runs on
deekshitvegi.com. Nothing in it refers to the portfolio. Written 1 Nov 2026 from the owner's
idea; the names and the plot are proposals until the owner signs them off.

## The premise

In 2161 the Earth's magnetic field was in the middle of a reversal, down to a tenth of its
strength, when the Sun let go a superflare (Kepler has seen flares a thousand times the 1859
Carrington event on stars like the Sun). With no field to turn it, the blast stripped the ozone
layer. Ultraviolet sterilised the land within a season, the crops failed, and the cities
followed.

The last ship out, the ark *Halcyon*, went through the wormhole beside Saturn (the Makers left
it there; nobody knows why) and came out by TON 618. One world there can hold life: **Haven**,
orbiting deep in the black hole's gravity. Time runs slow on Haven. **One day there is ten years
at home.**

That one fact makes the story work:

- The colonists have lived a month on Haven. Three hundred years have passed on Earth.
- The field came back. Life in the deep sea had ridden it out, as it rode out every mass
  extinction, and it has crept back onto the land: lichen on the rocks, algae in the shallows,
  a few hardy animals. Life found a way. But the ozone is thin, the soil is ash, and nothing
  people eat grows there. It is not ready for them.
- Haven is failing too. Its orbit is decaying towards the horizon.
- So a crew of four goes back through the wormhole in the *Argo*, the *Halcyon*'s long-range
  tender, to finish what life started and call the *Halcyon* home. They live at the Solar System's pace. If the job takes them eight years,
  their families on Haven will have waited less than a day. Nobody back home will ever know
  what it cost them. That is the heart of it, the way it is in *Interstellar*.

## The crew

Four people, all playable. Switch between them at any time (as in GTA V): each stays where you
left them, on whatever world, carrying on with what they were doing.

| | Name | Role | What they are best at |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Dev Arora** | Commander, pilot | Flying: longer pulse jumps, the autopilot, landing in storms |
| 2 | **Dr. Noor Haddad** | Botanist | Growing: crops grow faster, reads the soil, counts for most of Earth's recovery |
| 3 | **Kai Mendes** | Engineer | Building: habitats, repairs, the rover; salvages more from wrecks |
| 4 | **Dr. Amara Okafor** | Physicist | The Makers' technology: reads glyphs, builds the stasis lance, runs the wormhole |

**ARGO** goes with them: the *Argo*'s mind, the voice already in the game (the ship and her
computer go by one name). It is also the assistant the player can ask anything (below).

## The Argo, and the way through

The *Argo* is the big ship (320 m: a spine, four engines, a turning habitat ring, a docking
collar in the hub's nose). She waits in the sky over the landing site. Each of the four has a
fighter of their own that can go anywhere in the Solar System, land anywhere, and be left
anywhere; but the wormhole's throat would tear a fighter apart. **Only the Argo can cross.** Fly a
fighter up to her bay, slow down and press U: it is drawn in and clamped, and from then on you
fly her. U again lets it go. She cannot land: undock and fly the fighter down. Getting her to
the wormhole (and keeping her safe while the crew is down on a world) is part of the game.

## Earth comes back

Earth's recovery is the game's main progress bar, 0 to 100%, and it shows on the planet itself.

| Stage | Recovery | What the player sees |
|---|---|---|
| Ash | 0-10% | Grey ground, dead trunks, ash storms, murky seas; lichen on the rocks |
| Lichen | 10-30% | Moss and lichen spreading, algae blooms along the coasts |
| Meadow | 30-55% | Grass and shrubs come back, the first insects |
| Forest | 55-80% | Trees, clear rivers, birds |
| Wild | 80-95% | Herds, predators, rain clouds, a blue sky again |
| Home | 95-100% | Air people can breathe unaided: the *Halcyon* can land |

Each of chapters 2 to 8 brings back one of the **Seven Seeds**, the things Earth is missing (the
travel-from-world-to-world structure of *Dragon Ball GT*, nothing else from it). Planting,
building greenhouses on Earth and releasing animals add to the bar between missions.

## Friends and enemies

- **Friends:** the Aeronauts (Jupiter's floaters), the Europans, and in the end the memory of the Makers.
- **Enemies:** the Hunters (Jupiter's predators), the methane-sea stalkers on Titan, Mars's dust
  storms, and the planets themselves: storms, heat, cold, radiation, vacuum.
- **Fights** are a science crew's fights: drive off, stun, freeze, outrun. The **stasis lance**
  (Amara builds it from Maker technology) wraps a target in slowed time for a few seconds. It
  works on predators, on falling rock and on a geyser about to blow. (The *Dr. Stone* idea of
  science as a weapon, with our own device.)
- **Weather hurts:** a tornado throws the ship and the explorer about and can wreck a habitat;
  lightning can strike; hail and dust wear down the shield. Shelter matters.

## Building and growing

- **Habitats** on any world with ground: a dome, then modules (greenhouse, solar array, oxygen
  extractor, workshop, landing pad). A crew member left at a habitat keeps it running.
- **Growing:** plant, water, wait, harvest. What grows depends on the light and the dome's air.
  Food feeds the crew; seeds and saplings go to Earth.
- **Crafting** grows into a tech tree, the *Dr. Stone* way: crystals and salvage into tools,
  tools into machines.

## ARGO, the assistant

Press a key and ask ARGO anything: where to go next, how to do something, what the gravity is
on Ganymede, what Noor is doing right now.

- **Built in, offline and free:** ARGO knows the game: every objective, every world's real
  figures, every recipe, every crew member's state. This answers most of what a player asks,
  with no server and no cost.
- **Online (optional):** open questions go to a language model through a small server the
  owner runs. The key stays on the server; the game never carries one. It costs money per
  question, so it needs a daily limit per player. Inside the Claude app preview it cannot reach
  any server, so the preview has the offline ARGO only.
